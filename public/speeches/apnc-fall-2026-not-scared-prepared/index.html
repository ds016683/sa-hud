Not Scared, Prepared.

Data, AI, and the Future of the Addiction Field
Artificial intelligence has arrived in behavioral health, and the conversation about it swings between hype and fear. Neither serves the field.
David Smith · Third Horizon · APNC Fall Conference · October 20, 2026
Fall Conference 2026
This Session

Three things you should be able to do when we are done.

  1. Describe how connected, real-time data on the workforce, funding, policy, and community conditions can inform daily practice, program decisions, and advocacy.
  2. Identify common misconceptions about artificial intelligence in behavioral health and distinguish what AI tools can and cannot do without professional expertise.
  3. Distinguish single-tool AI solutions, such as chatbots, from integrated data platforms, and describe what platform-based approaches mean for North Carolina's addiction workforce.

What It Means for the Field

People. Information. Machines. Finding the balance.

The field is going to have to find the right balance between people, information, and machines, and how those elements come together to maximize an output.
01People

The professionals who carry the work, and the supervisors, managers, and state stewards behind them. Everything here exists to make them better at the job.

02Information

Real North Carolina data: the state's own deliverables, the credential registry, the statewide assessment, opioid settlement dollars, and workforce intelligence all in one place.

03Machines

Technology that synthesizes data like never before. It enhances the abilities of the people who do the work. It does not replace them.

The information informs the people. The people sharpen the information. The machines carry both further.

What It Means for the Field

Three domains, three questions.

Administration

Am I paying others for administrative services today that I may now be able to perform myself?

For example
  • Billing, claims, and prior auth
  • Scheduling, intake, and reminders
  • HR, compliance, and grant reporting

Business

What information or tools have I wanted to have at my fingertips to manage my responsibilities that I haven't ever been able to access?

For example
  • What payers actually pay, by service
  • Where referrals come from and go
  • Workforce supply and funding flows, by county

Clinical

How can these tools be integrated with the deeply human work to expand the toolset that leads to recovery, healing, and/or progression?

For example
  • Notes that write themselves while you stay present
  • Patterns across a caseload you could not see alone
  • Support between sessions, on the client's terms
